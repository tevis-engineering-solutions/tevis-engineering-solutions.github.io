How it works

From the state's crash portal to a lead list you own

The tool searches Ohio's official Crash Retrieval portal by county or law-enforcement agency, reads every injury report with AI, leaves out drivers who were charged with an offense, and de-duplicates everyone into a database on your own computer. You only ever solve CAPTCHAs. Everything else is automatic.

Why the one human step? Ohio's portal puts a CAPTCHA on every search so a person, not a bot, makes each request. We deliberately do not build CAPTCHA-defeating automation: it is brittle, it works against the site's controls, and it is the wrong exposure for a professional practice. Want it fully unattended? Add a paid, contracted data feed as an upgrade.
  1. Pick your scope

    Choose counties on the map or one law-enforcement agency, and a date window.

  2. Clear one check

    A staff member clears one quick anti-bot check per search, a few minutes for a multi-county pull.

  3. Fetch and read

    The tool pages the results, downloads the injury reports, reads each one into records, and sets aside cited drivers.

  4. Work the leads

    De-duplicated leads land on the map dashboard, ready to filter, search, enrich and export.

Contact enrichment

Phone and email, only for the leads you choose

An Ohio crash report gives you a name and a mailing address. It almost never gives you a phone number. Contact enrichment closes that gap through People Data Labs, a third-party identity provider, on a budget you set.

  1. Filter

    Narrow the dashboard to the leads worth reaching: county, date window, injury level, drivers who were not cited.

  2. Package

    One click bundles that exact list and tells you how many are in it, how many were already looked up, and the most it can cost.

  3. Enrich

    Each person is looked up at the match confidence you set. Anyone already done is skipped, and the run stops at your cap.

  4. Reach out

    Phones and emails land in the dashboard and a contacts export, ready for your CRM, mail house or ad platform.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Where does the crash data come from?
From Ohio's own official public Crash Retrieval portal, operated by the Ohio Department of Public Safety. The tool reads the same public OH-1 crash reports anyone can request — it does not hack, scrape around security, or defeat the site's anti-bot check. A person clears one quick CAPTCHA per search, and everything after that is automated. If you want the manual version of that process, we wrote it up in how to get an Ohio crash report.
Are phone numbers and emails included?
Not by default. Phone and email are almost never printed on a crash report, so the base output is an addressable audience (name, mailing address, injury, county, agency, date) — the same shape as the broker spreadsheets, which sell contacts as a paid “append.” When you want them, contact enrichment adds phones and emails for only the leads you select. On the software, it runs on your own People Data Labs account: PDL bills you directly, charges only for matches, and you set a cap on every run. On a subscription or Data as a Service, we can handle it for you instead and bill on usage — the contact data your leads actually use plus a modest service fee — up to a monthly limit you choose. Either way it is optional; the tool works fully without it. See what is actually on an OH-1 for the full field list.
How much does it cost to run?
On the own-it option the only per-use cost is AI — about 0.2¢ per injury report read, a few dollars a month for a weekly regional pull. The subscription includes the AI in the monthly price. A rules-only mode runs the whole pipeline for $0.
How should we handle compliance?
The tool reads public records through the state's own public portal and respects its controls. What you do with the data afterwards is yours to determine: every field has its own rules about outreach, and they differ by industry and change over time. Do your own due diligence with your own counsel and use the tool inside the policies that govern your practice. We are happy to build filters, suppression lists, and cadence controls that fit whatever those policies turn out to be. Contact enrichment adds phone and email data, which brings outreach rules of its own; the same advice applies, and we’ll set the holds and suppression to match.
