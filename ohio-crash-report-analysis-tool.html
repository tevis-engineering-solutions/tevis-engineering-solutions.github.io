The tool scans Ohio's official crash-report portal — by county or by law-enforcement agency — over a rolling date window; uses AI to read each OH-1 report and extract every person involved; de-duplicates them into a database; and presents a live dashboard plus a one-click spreadsheet export.

What the Ohio crash report tool does

County + agency search

An interactive map and searchable list for all 88 Ohio counties, with one-click presets (NE Ohio, big metros, all 88). Or query a law-enforcement agency — one search pulls that agency's injury crashes statewide.

AI extraction, your way

Reads each report PDF and returns one record per person (driver, passenger, pedestrian) with name, address, age, gender, and injury severity. A mode switch trades cost for accuracy: hybrid, always-AI, or rules-only ($0).

De-duplication & storage

A local database collapses the same person across multiple crashes into one audience entry, while keeping full per-crash provenance — and remembers your last scan for glance-and-go weekly refreshes.

Lead dashboard

A live, filterable list (county, severity), a “hide No Apparent Injury” toggle, per-lead link to the source PDF, per-county counts, and a severity breakdown — all exportable to CSV in one click.

Live cost meter

A running read-out of the AI spend for the session as it happens. You hold the throttle — the AI runs on a key you control, capped by you, and rules-only mode runs the entire pipeline for $0.

Your data stays yours

On the own-it option the database never leaves your office. The audience is exclusive — you generate and own it — unlike a broker list resold to every competitor in your market.

Why the one human step? Ohio's portal places a CAPTCHA on every search to ensure a person — not a bot — makes each request. We deliberately do not build CAPTCHA-defeating automation: it is brittle, works against the site's controls, and is the wrong exposure for a professional practice. Free ⇒ accept a small human step each run. Fully unattended ⇒ add a paid, contracted data feed (available as an upgrade).

Frequently Asked Questions

Where does the crash data come from?
From Ohio's own official public Crash Retrieval portal, operated by the Ohio Department of Public Safety. The tool reads the same public OH-1 crash reports anyone can request — it does not hack, scrape around security, or defeat the site's anti-bot check. A person clears one quick CAPTCHA per search, and everything after that is automated. If you want the manual version of that process, we wrote it up in how to get an Ohio crash report.
Are phone numbers and emails included?
No. Phone and email are not printed on a crash report, so they are not in the base output — exactly like the broker spreadsheets, which sell them as a paid “append.” The tool produces an addressable audience (name, mailing address, injury, county, agency, date). Optional phone / email enrichment can be added through the same third-party identity providers the brokers use. See what is actually on an OH-1 for the full field list.
How much does it cost to run?
On the own-it option the only per-use cost is AI — about 1.5¢ per injury report read, roughly $10–$25/month for a weekly regional pull. The subscription includes the AI in the monthly price. A rules-only mode runs the whole pipeline for $0.
How should we handle compliance?
The tool reads public records through the state's own public portal and respects its controls. What you do with the data afterwards is yours to determine: every field has its own rules about outreach, and they differ by industry and change over time. Do your own due diligence with your own counsel and use the tool inside the policies that govern your practice. We are happy to build filters, suppression lists, and cadence controls that fit whatever those policies turn out to be.
