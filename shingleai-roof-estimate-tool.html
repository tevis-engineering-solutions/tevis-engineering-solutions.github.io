    Frequently Asked Questions

    Does the homeowner have to draw their own roof?
    Usually not. Where aerial survey data covers the building, the roof is measured for them — pitch-corrected area, the measured pitch, and the number of roof planes — and the outline is drawn on the satellite view so they only confirm it is the right house. Hand-tracing is the fallback for buildings the survey does not cover.
    How accurate is the estimate?
    Accurate enough to price a conversation, not to sign a contract — and the widget says so directly under the number. It corrects the footprint to the true roof surface using the measured pitch (a 6/12 roof is 11.8% larger than its footprint; a 12/12 is 41.4% larger), and it excludes flat areas it believes are decks or porches and tells the homeowner it did. It cannot see decking, flashing, ventilation, or how many layers are already up there, so the output is deliberately a range and every path ends on booking an on-site measurement.
    Will it work on my existing website?
    In most cases, yes. The estimator is delivered as a responsive iframe plus a small sizing script, which works on WordPress, Squarespace, Wix, and custom sites. TES handles the install rather than leaving you a code snippet to figure out.
    Who owns the leads?
    You do. Leads are emailed straight to your inbox from your own branded estimator on your own website. They are not sold, shared, or resold to other contractors in your market — which is the whole point of running your own estimator instead of buying from a lead marketplace.
    Can my competitors see my pricing?
    No. Your rate card never reaches the browser. The page sends the roof geometry to the server and the server sends back dollars, so there is nothing to read in view-source. Your estimator is also locked to your own domains, so nobody can embed it on another site and collect your leads.
    What does it cost, and who pays Google?
    Setup is quoted per contractor — the two things that move the number are how complex your rate card is and where your website is hosted. Google Maps and Solar usage runs on your own Google Cloud account at cost, on keys we create in your name with restrictions and a spending cap you control. TES never marks it up.
    What happens if a roof is not covered by the survey data?
    The estimator falls back to a hand trace: the homeowner outlines the roof on the aerial and picks a pitch, and the footprint is corrected to a surface area from there. Every lead is labeled measured or traced — in the app, in the lead record, and in your email — so you always know which you are looking at before you drive out.
