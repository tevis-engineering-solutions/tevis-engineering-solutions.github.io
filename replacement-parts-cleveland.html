How We Rebuild a Part

Every replacement is measured, modeled and test-fit before the final print. You keep the files.

  1. Measure

    Send photos first. We measure the original, its pieces or the part it fits into with calipers.

  2. Model

    We rebuild it in CAD and make a dimensioned drawing, fixing the weak spot that broke if we can.

  3. Fit-test

    We print a test piece and check it against the original or the mating part, then adjust.

  4. Print

    The final part is printed in the material that suits where it lives. You get the STL and STEP files.

The Right Material for Where It Lives

Tell us where the part goes and what it has to survive. The material usually picks itself.

  • PLARigid and easy to print. Good for indoor parts that never see heat; it softens in a hot car or a sunny window.
  • PETGTougher, slightly flexible, with better heat and moisture resistance. Our usual choice for working brackets and covers.
  • TPURubbery and flexible, for gaskets, bumpers, feet and grips.

What to Send Us

The more of this you have, the faster the quote.

  • Photos of the part from several sides, with a ruler in the shot
  • Every broken piece, even the small ones
  • The make and model of what it came from
  • The part it attaches to, if it can come off
  • Where it lives: indoors, outdoors, near heat or water
  • How many you need

If printing is the wrong answer, we will say so. Parts that carry real load, sit near engine heat or hold pressure may need a different material or a machined part. You hear that before you pay for anything.

Replacement Part Questions

How much does a replacement part cost?
Recreating a broken or discontinued part starts at $400 plus material. That covers measuring, modeling and test-fitting. If you already have a 3D file, printing starts at $40 plus material. Extra copies of a part we have already modeled cost only the printing and material.
Can you make a part from photos?
Often, for simple parts, if the photos include a ruler or calipers. For anything that has to fit precisely, we measure the original or the part it mates with.
The part broke into pieces. Can you still copy it?
Usually. Keep every piece, even the small ones. We measure what is left, fill in the missing geometry from the parts it connects to, and confirm the fit with a test print.
Do I get the CAD file?
Yes. You get the STL and STEP files, so the part can be printed again later by us or anyone else.
Can you make more than one?
Yes. Once a part is modeled, extra copies are just printing and material. Short runs of 5 to 25 are quoted on request.
Is a 3D printed part strong enough?
For most knobs, clips, covers, brackets and fixtures, yes, in the right material. If a part carries real load, sits near heat or holds pressure, we will tell you before you pay whether printing is the right answer.
