What it checks

Twenty-Eight Objective Checks, Six Categories

Every check is something a person could verify by hand in a browser. Nothing here is a matter of taste, and nothing is a guess about your design. If we say your call button does not dial, you can tap it and see.

Revenue-blocking

The expensive ones. A site that does not load, a broken security certificate, a Call button that navigates instead of dialing, a phone number that displays one line and dials another, a contact form embedded from nowhere, or no way to reach you at all.

Trust-breaking

Stock photography presented as your own work, placeholder client logos that shipped with a template, review badges that are dated screenshots, social previews carrying another company’s branding, and missing address or license details.

Staleness

A copyright year that says you closed, and a photo gallery whose newest upload is years old. The first thing a careful buyer checks is the bottom of the page.

Local-SEO invisibility

A page title that says “Home”, no city or service area named anywhere, a free webmail address on a business that owns a domain, and social buttons that link to Facebook itself instead of your page.

Polish

Template placeholder copy nobody replaced, unrendered template code showing on the page, and a short list of misspellings that turn up on trade sites over and over.

Technical health

Mobile viewport, pinch-to-zoom left disabled, missing meta description or main heading, insecure files loaded on a secure page, and a slow first response. Plus Google’s own speed measurement of your site on a phone.

How it works

Three Steps, No Catch

You enter your address

Just the website. Add a note about anything you want us to focus on, or leave it blank. We do not ask for your email, because we do not need it to run the scan.

We read your live pages

Our server loads your homepage and a couple of linked pages, runs every check, and asks Google to measure your speed on a phone. Read-only. Nothing on your site changes.

Your report opens

A graded report card in a new tab, with every finding, the evidence behind it, and a written plan for what to fix first. Save it as a PDF or send it to whoever maintains your site.

Why we give it away: we already run this audit before contacting anyone, so showing you the result costs us nothing we were not already spending. If the report is useful and you fix things yourself, good. If you would rather hand it to somebody, we are here. Both outcomes are fine, which is why the findings are yours either way.
What you get

Inside The Report

A letter grade, and the arithmetic behind it

Every site starts at 100. Each defect subtracts a fixed amount by severity, so the grade is explainable rather than an opinion. You also get a per-category score, so you can see whether the problem is trust, staleness, or something actively blocking revenue.

Every finding, with the evidence

Not “improve your SEO”. The exact button that does not dial, the exact year in your footer, the exact stock photo URL. Each one comes with what it costs you in plain business terms and the fix we would apply.

Google’s own speed numbers

Performance, accessibility, SEO and best-practices scores from PageSpeed Insights, plus the timing metrics translated out of jargon. Where Google has enough real Chrome traffic on your site, you also get what actual visitors experienced.

A written plan, in our words

The closing section reads the findings and writes up what to fix first, why it matters for your business, and what we would do about it. If you left us a note or a question, it answers that directly. It only discusses what the scan actually measured.

After the report

Most findings have a fix a competent web person can apply in an afternoon. The report names each one specifically enough to hand to whoever built your site. We would rather you fix it than leave it broken, regardless of who does the work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it really free, and is there a catch?
It is free and there is no catch. No email address, no account, and nothing is added to a mailing list. We already run this audit on businesses before we contact them, so running it for you costs us nothing we were not already spending. The findings are yours whether or not you ever call us.
Will this change anything on my website?
No. The scan is read-only. It loads your pages the way any visitor's browser would, reads the HTML, and closes the connection. It never logs in, never submits a form, never uploads anything, and never stores copies of your pages.
What can it not see?
Anything behind a login, anything that only appears after a visitor clicks something, and design quality, which is a matter of judgment rather than measurement. It reads your homepage and a couple of linked pages, so a defect buried five pages deep may not surface. Everything it does report, it measured.
Do you keep my website address or my notes?
The report itself is built in your own browser and stored there, not on our servers. Your address and any note you write are sent to our server to run the scan and write the recommendations, and are not added to any marketing list. See our privacy policy for the full picture.
My site got a bad grade. Is it really that bad?
The grade is arithmetic, not judgment: every site starts at 100 and each defect subtracts a fixed amount by severity. A single critical problem, like a call button that does not dial, drops a grade hard because that one defect really does cost you customers. Read the individual findings rather than the letter.
Who is behind this?
Tevis Engineering Solutions, a veteran-owned IT and engineering firm in Cleveland, Ohio, run by Tyler Tevis. The scanner is our own tool, built in-house for our outreach work, and this is the browser version of it.
